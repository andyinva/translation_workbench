# Candidates for transliteration (proposal, 2026-10-25)

Status: proposed list, nothing decided except *yovel*. Counts are Old Testament uses in the source database. The KJV column comes from Word Atlas (the Strong's dictionary). Tests are the five tests of the merged rule: 1 English gap or misleading word, 2 closed-set item, 3 links passages that English separates, 4 identity or meaning uncertain, 5 the text plays on or explains the word.

Strength: **strong** (passes the tests clearly), **maybe** (passes but is a cluster or judgment risk), **rule** (a measure, covered by an existing rule), **familiar** (English already has this form), **hold** (frequent, decide separately).

## How it would look (density)

The whole Old Testament has about 305,500 words. The numbers below count each occurrence once.

| Set | Lemmas | Occurrences | Share of all words | Chapters touched | Verses with 3 or more terms | Heaviest chapters |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A: strong only | 30 | 790 | 0.26% (1 word in 380) | 319 of 929 | 14 | Lev 25 (26), Lev 13 (21), Josh 6 (19), Lev 27 (18) |
| B: A plus familiar and measures | 55 | 1,390 | 0.45% (1 in 220) | 438 | 50 | Lev 25 (34), Lev 27 (32), Ezek 45 (31), Num 7 (28) |
| C: B plus maybe | 86 | 2,317 | 0.76% (1 in 130) | 548 | 123 | Num 7 (95), Num 29 (51), Num 28 (47), Ezek 45 (43) |

Of the 790 occurrences in set A, 221 are words the KJV already leaves in Hebrew form (Selah, manna, ephod, Urim and Thummim and others), and 569 are new to a KJV reader. The new ones: yovel 27, shofar 72, hevel 73, kapporet 27, tevah 28, Azazel 4, nephilim 3, Shaddai 48, tachash 14, bedolach 2, tsaraat 35, tannin 26, Sheol 66, goel 104, asherah 40.

Reading: set A is light (about one word in 380, and most chapters have none). The risk is in clusters, not in the average: Genesis has terms in 16 of its 50 chapters (set A), Ecclesiastes 1:2 has five *hevel* in one verse, Ruth 4:4 has five *goel*, and the offering chapters (Num 7, 28, 29, Lev 2) and stone lists (Ezek 28:13 has eight names in a verse) would be thick in set C.

## Suggested trial

Start with set A and judge it on five chapters where terms cluster: Leviticus 25 (yovel), Joshua 6 (yovel, shofar), Ruth 4 (goel), Ecclesiastes 1 (hevel), Exodus 28 (ephod, Urim and Thummim, stones). Keep set B and C out until the first look.

Safeguards against losing the reader: a gloss note at the first use of each term in each book; a printed list of all transliterated terms; and a level flag (core, optional) on each term so the same database can print a reading edition with the English word and a study edition with the transliteration.
